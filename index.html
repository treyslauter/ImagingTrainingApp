How the image is calculated

Spin echo: S = PD · (1 − e−TR/T1) · e−TE/T2

Inversion recovery: S = PD · (1 − 2e−TI/T1 + e−TR/T1) · e−TE/T2. Magnitude reconstruction shows |S|, so a tissue is black when its TI lands on its zero-crossing. The null point is TI ≈ T1 · ln 2 when TR ≫ T1.

Spoiled gradient echo: S = PD · sin α · (1 − E1) / (1 − cos α · E1) · e−TE/T2*, where E1 = e−TR/T1. There is no 180° refocusing pulse, so decay follows T2*, not T2.

SNR scales with voxel volume × √(phase steps × NEX ÷ bandwidth) × field strength. Scan time = TR × phase steps × NEX ÷ ETL.

Artifacts modeled: Gibbs ringing and blurring come from truncating k-space to the chosen matrix. Wraparound appears when the FOV is smaller than the head in the phase direction. Fat shifts along the frequency axis by (3.5 ppm × 42.58 MHz/T × B0) ÷ (bandwidth per pixel). Motion is modeled as random shifts between phase-encode lines, which produces ghosting in the phase direction. Noise is Rician because the image is displayed as magnitude.

Not modeled: FSE blurring from the echo train, magnetization transfer, B1 and coil inhomogeneity, flow, susceptibility, and slice-profile effects. On FSE, fat is made brighter to mimic J-coupling. The tissue values are typical literature figures and are rounded for teaching. The anatomy is an average of 152 brains, so cortical detail is a little smoother than in a single patient. Each stored slab is 3 mm, and thicker slices blend neighboring slabs, so partial-volume averaging is real.

Sinogram (raw projection data)

Each row is one projection angle (0–180°, top to bottom), and each column is one detector channel. Every point in the body traces a sine wave. Filtered backprojection turns this back into the image. A bad detector shows up as a vertical line, metal as a bright sine wave, and motion as a jump partway down.

CT numbers depend on kVp

Water is always 0 HU because scanners calibrate it at each kVp. Iodine and bone have higher atomic numbers, so their HU rise at low kVp (more photoelectric absorption).

How this CT is simulated

Anatomy: each voxel of a real CT is split into water-equivalent tissue and bone-equivalent material. Their attenuation is then recalculated for every energy in the X-ray spectrum.

Acquisition: parallel-beam projections over 180°. Each detector reading is a sum over the spectrum of e−∫μ(E), with Poisson photon noise proportional to mAs ÷ pitch × slice thickness.

Reconstruction: −ln(I/I₀), optional water beam-hardening correction, a ramp filter shaped by the kernel, then backprojection. HU = 1000 × (μ − μwater) / μwater.

Simplified: parallel beam instead of fan or cone beam, no bowtie filter or tube current modulation, and no helical interpolation. "Iterative" is an edge-preserving denoising stand-in. Dose numbers are typical scanner values for illustration.

X-ray spectrum (after filtration)

Bremsstrahlung plus tungsten characteristic lines, filtered by 3 mm aluminium. kVp sets the maximum energy and shifts the mean. mAs changes only the number of photons, not their energies.

How this radiograph is simulated

Primary beam: rays from the focal spot pass through the CT volume in 10 depth slabs. Each slab is magnified by its own SID ÷ (SID − distance to detector), so AP and PA geometry differ realistically. Transmission is summed over the spectrum for water-like and bone-like material.

Scatter: a broad blur proportional to irradiated tissue thickness and field size. The grid passes ~72% of primary and ~11% of scatter.

Detector: air kerma ∝ kVp² × mAs ÷ SID², with Poisson noise. The exposure index (EI) is 100 × median detector air kerma in µGy, with a target of 250. DI = 10·log₁₀(EI / 250).

Processing: digital mode log-transforms the signal, rescales it from the histogram and applies a contrast curve and unsharp masking. Screen-film follows a fixed H&D curve, so exposure errors show as light or dark films.

Anatomy: ICBM 152 Nonlinear Asymmetric 2009c template (Fonov et al., NeuroImage 2011; Fonov et al., NeuroImage 2009), via TemplateFlow. Brain tissue comes from the template's gray matter, white matter and CSF probability maps. Fat, bone/air, muscle and fluid outside the brain are estimated from the template's T1w and T2w intensities. The lesions are simulated.
